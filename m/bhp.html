Biogas Heat Pump (BHP) · 30–480 kW

When the boiler stops, the cooling does not.

A mill’s air-conditioning usually hangs off one spine: boiler, steam turbine, mill grid, electric chillers. Stop the boiler and the offices, laboratory and worker housing cook — just when the mill needs them most. The BHP system breaks that dependency. A small gas engine drives the compressor directly, and the fuel is 97% biomethane made from the mill’s own biogas. There is no electric motor in the drive train, no grid connection and no diesel.

The single point of failure

One stop, four stops

This is the chain that decides whether a mill has air-conditioning on any given day. Every link is electrically or thermally dependent on the one before it.

BoilerBiomass or fibre fired; stops for maintenance, fuel shortage or a boiler trip.

Steam turbineNo steam, no shaft power, no generation.

Mill gridLoad shedding follows within seconds; priority goes to the process, not the offices.

Electric A/CCompressors stop. Rooms heat up within 20–30 minutes.

A diesel genset can bridge the gap, but it is the most expensive kWh on site — and its fuel is the first thing to run out when the road is flooded.

The BHP chain

Only the first link matters

BiogasFrom the covered lagoon or digester — the mill already owns it.

Gas engine97% methane, spark ignition, ~30% shaft efficiency.

CompressorDirect-coupled or belt drive; no motor, no inverter, no grid.

CoolingKeeps working through a boiler shutdown, a grid outage or a diesel shortage.

The unit

A packaged outdoor unit, not a plant room

The engine, compressor, condenser, gas train, starter battery and controller arrive on one skid, factory run-tested. Set it on a plinth, connect the gas line, the chilled-water or refrigerant lines and a small control cable. No motor control centre, no soft starter, no transformer upgrade.

  • Cooling 30–480 kW per unit; up to 8 units in parallel
  • Gas inlet 5–20 kPa; 97% CH₄; H₂S below 10 ppm
  • Engine waste heat recovered to 60–90 °C hot water — optional, and often the best-paying part
  • About 65 dB(A) at 10 m — no need for a separate acoustic enclosure
  • Floor-mounted or roof-mounted; 1.2–2.2 t depending on model
Gas in, cold air out, hot water as a by-product

How it works

The engine does mechanical work on the compressor — it is not an absorption chiller. That is why one Nm³ of methane gives roughly 10 kWh of cooling, about 1.5 times what a single-effect absorption chiller extracts from the same gas.

1 · Gas97% methane at 5–20 kPa, filtered and regulated. H₂S and siloxanes are removed upstream, in the upgrading plant.

2 · EngineSpark-ignition gas engine, 1,500 or 1,800 rpm, driving through a coupling or belt.

3 · CompressionOpen-drive reciprocating or scroll compressor; capacity modulated by engine speed and cylinder unloading.

4 · RefrigerantCondenser rejects heat to outdoor air; expansion valve; evaporator cools water or air for the rooms.

5 · HeatJacket water and exhaust heat go to a heat exchanger: 60–90 °C hot water for the mill, canteen or hostel.

The COP quoted here is on fuel LHV, the way gas heat pumps are rated in Japan. On an electrical-equivalent basis the compressor itself runs at a COP of about 3.0 — the same as a good electric chiller; what changes is where the shaft power comes from.

A mature Japanese platform, re-calibrated for biomethane

Technology origin

Gas-engine heat pumps were developed and industrialised by Sanyo’s air-conditioning division in Japan. The same class of machine is built in China by Dalian Sino-Tech Precision (Sino-Tech). BHP takes that proven mechanical architecture — gas engine, open-drive compressor, engine heat recovery — and adapts the fuel system to 97% biomethane from palm oil mill waste.

01 · Proven architecture

Engine, not absorption

Mechanical compression gives about 1.5× the cooling of a single-effect absorption chiller on the same gas input, with no lithium bromide, no corrosion loop and no crystallization risk.

02 · What we change

Biogas-specific fuel system

Gas train sized for 97% methane, air/fuel ratio and ignition timing remapped, knock control and derating for methane-number variation, biogas-compatible oil and service intervals.

03 · What stays

The parts that made it last

Industrial reciprocating engine, open-drive compressor, copper-tube aluminium-fin coils, service access from the front. Overhaul at 20,000–30,000 h; 15-year design life.

Reference machines of this class include Panasonic GHP outdoor units and Dalian Sino-Tech Precision (Sino-Tech) gas-engine heat pump packages. Worked example · 120 kW

About two years against diesel

A 120 kW BHP unit carries the office and laboratory load. Against diesel-generated electricity it saves about RM 162,000 a year. At an indicative RM 2,500/kW for the equipment, payback is about 1.8 years — before any grant or carbon credit.

When it is not the answer

Reliable grid, cheap power

If the mill has firm grid power at RM 0.35–0.45/kWh and the boiler never stops, an electric VRF system is cheaper to buy and cheaper to run. BHP earns its place where the grid is absent or unreliable, where the turbine stops, or where diesel is the cooling fuel today. Be honest about which case you are in.
