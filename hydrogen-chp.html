Hydrogen CHP · 7.5–100 kW

Bottled biomethane in, power and hot water out.

Methane reforming plus a fuel cell: the same architecture as Japan's household fuel-cell CHP, scaled to estate housing, resorts, mills and off-grid communities. Two feeds work: bottled 97% biomethane, or raw 60% biogas taken straight from the mill with an integrated purification front end. Either way the methane is reformed on site into hydrogen and converted to electricity and 60–70 °C hot water — no pipeline, no diesel, no flame at the point of use.

Six zeros

Nothing leaves this unit but water.

No burner, no engine, no flue in the usual sense. This is the strongest reason to choose reforming plus a fuel cell over a diesel or gas engine — and it is measurable at the fence line, not a marketing line.

01 · No noise

No engine, no burner

The only moving parts are a blower and pumps. About 55 dB(A) at one metre — quieter than a normal conversation, so the unit can stand beside staff housing.

02 · No nitrogen oxides

NOx = 0

A fuel cell runs at 60–200 °C. With no high-temperature flame, thermal NOx cannot form at all — it is below detection, not merely “low”.

03 · No sulphur oxides

SOx = 0

Gas is desulphurised to below 1 ppm before the reformer. No sulphur enters the process, so none can leave it — no SO₂, no acid smell, no corrosion downstream.

04 · No dust or particulates

PM = 0

Gas fuel, sealed process, no combustion air and no flue: no particulates, no black smoke, no smell — unlike diesel exhaust, which is a known health hazard.

05 · Pure water only

H₂O

The electrochemical reaction produces water and heat. The water is clean enough to recover for washing or irrigation instead of discharging it.

06 · Near-zero CO₂

CO₂ ≈ 0

The carbon is short-cycle carbon from palm waste, and reforming gives a concentrated CO₂ stream that is far easier to capture than dilute flue gas. This is a path to zero, not just “lower”.

You can hear the difference at the fence line.

A diesel genset is the exact opposite on all six counts: 95–105 dB(A), NOx, SOx, black smoke and a fuel tank. Ask for the emissions sheet with the quotation.

Why this fits Malaysia

The fuel chain already exists — the generator was missing

Biogas is already being upgraded to 97% methane and sold in cylinders to households and shops. That means the hard part — gas cleaning, compression and distribution — is solved. What is missing is a quiet, low-maintenance generator that can burn that gas where it is used.

01 · Fuel

Bottled biomethane, no pipeline

A 50-litre cylinder at 200 bar holds about 10 Nm³ of methane — roughly 99 kWh of fuel, or 10 hours at 10 kW electrical. Cylinders can be swapped like LPG, so there is no gas network to build and no stranded asset if demand moves.

02 · Cost

Against diesel, not against the grid

Off-grid estates, islands and remote resorts in Sabah pay RM 0.90–1.30 per kWh for diesel power once fuel logistics, servicing and downtime are counted. That is the number this unit competes with — not the subsidised grid tariff.

03 · Heat

Hot water is half the output

A fuel cell makes electricity and heat at the same time: 42% electric, 43% recoverable as 60–70 °C water. Staff housing, kitchens, laundries, sterilisation and hotel hot water all use that heat directly, which is what lifts total efficiency to about 85%.

Two feed options

Bottled gas, or raw biogas straight from the mill

The reformer and fuel cell are the same in both cases. Only the front end changes — and it is built into the same package, not bought as a separate upgrading plant.

A · Bottled 97% biomethane

Lowest investment, fuel bought by the cylinder

Standard specification. The gas arrives at 200 bar already at 97% methane, so the unit only needs desulphurisation and filtration before the reformer. Nothing to build, nothing to operate upstream — but you pay the bottled-gas price for every Nm³, and you depend on the cylinder supply chain.

RM 10,000/kW · FOB, installation excluded

B · Raw biogas, about 60% CH₄

Straight from the palm oil mill — purification built in

Take the biogas directly off the mill pond and skip upgrading, bottling and trucking altogether. An integrated front-end purification module — desulphurisation, CO₂ removal, drying and compression — is supplied as part of our unit and lifts the gas to the ≥95% methane the reformer needs. Higher investment than option A, but the fuel is the mill’s own biogas instead of bought cylinders, and there is no separate upgrading plant to pay for.

RM 19,500/kW · FOB, integrated purification included in the unit

Both options use the same reformer and fuel cell; option B simply adds the purification front end inside the same enclosure or skid. The added cost depends on gas flow, H₂S and CO₂ content, so it is quoted per site after a gas analysis — send us your measured flow, methane content and H₂S and we will price both versions side by side.

Process

From methane to electrons in five steps

ConditionDesulphurisation to below 1 ppm H₂S, plus filtration. The reformer catalyst is poisoned by sulphur. On raw 60% biogas the same integrated module also strips CO₂ and water.
ReformSteam reforming at 650–800 °C: CH₄ + H₂O → CO + 3H₂, followed by water-gas shift.
PurifyPSA or membrane to 99.9% H₂; the off-gas is burned to heat the reformer, which is why efficiency holds.
ConvertPEM fuel cell at 65–75 °C, or PAFC at 160–200 °C for higher-grade heat.
Deliver230/400 V AC plus 60–70 °C hot water. Grid-tie or island mode, black-start capable.

No hydrogen is stored. Hydrogen is made on demand and consumed inside the unit — that is what keeps the safety case simple and avoids a pressure vessel on site.

FAQ

Questions we get asked

Can it run on raw biogas?

Yes — with the integrated purification front end. Raw POME biogas at about 60% methane is desulphurised, stripped of CO₂, dried and compressed inside the same package, then fed to the reformer at ≥95% CH₄. The reformer itself still needs concentrated methane; what changes is that we do the upgrading in the unit instead of you buying bottled gas. Investment is higher than the bottled-gas version and is quoted per site on gas flow, H₂S and CO₂.

Is hydrogen stored on site?

No. Hydrogen is produced on demand and consumed inside the unit within seconds. There is no hydrogen vessel, no high-pressure storage and no delivery of hydrogen.

What maintenance does it need?

Annual service, reformer catalyst every 3–5 years, fuel-cell stack every 5–8 years (25–35% of unit price). Budget 3% of capex a year and you will not be surprised.

Can several units run together?

Yes. Units parallel on the AC side up to 1 MW, and a failed unit only takes its share of the load offline — which is why we prefer 2 × 50 kW to 1 × 100 kW where the budget allows.
